OpenHamClock

A free, open-source dashboard in your web browser that puts what's happening on the air on one screen: space weather, live spots, propagation for your location, parks and summits, DXpeditions and contests. openhamclock.com · no account, no download, no ads.

Getting started

  1. Open openhamclock.com in any browser: computer, tablet or phone.
  2. Enter your callsign and grid square. "Use my current location" fills in the grid for you.
  3. You're on the Modern layout (the default). Others: Settings → Display → Layout.
  4. Click your callsign (top left) to open Settings at any time.

Settings are saved in your browser, on that device. To install it like an app: iPhone Safari Share → Add to Home Screen; Android Chrome ⋮ → Install app; Chrome/Edge on a computer, the install icon in the address bar.

Rule of thumb High SFI and low K = a good HF day.

1Header: clocks and space weather

UTC is the time used by every log, spot, contest and DXpedition (EDT = UTC−4; EST = UTC−5). Click LOCAL to switch 12/24 hour. Click your callsign for Settings, the version for what's new.

What it is How to read it (app colors)
SFI Solar Flux Index: the sun's radio output at 10.7 cm; a stand-in for how hard it's ionizing the upper atmosphere Higher is better, especially 15–10 m. ~70 quiet sun · 120+ good · 150+ excellent
K Planetary K-index: geomagnetic disturbance, 0–9, over 3 hours Low is better. 0–2 quiet. 4+ shows red: absorption, noise, aurora
SSN Sunspot number Rises and falls with SFI over the 11-year cycle
A A-index: the whole day's geomagnetic disturbance in one number <10 green · 10–19 amber · 20+ red
SW Solar wind speed, km/s <500 green · 500–699 amber · 700+ red. Fast wind stirs up the field
Word Geomagnetic field summary from N0NBH (HamQSL) QUIET green · ACTIVE or any STORM red · anything else amber

2DE: your station

  • "DE" is telegraph shorthand for "from": your end.
  • Grid square, e.g. FN20nc: FN = 20°×10° field, 20 = 2°×1° square, nc = a few miles. Most of Lower Bucks is FN20.
  • Lat/long: every distance and heading is measured from here.
  • Sunrise → sunset (local). Low bands improve after dark; gray-line openings happen around these times.

2DX: your target

  • Local time at their end
  • Beam Dir: short-path great-circle heading. Japan from here is ~332°, over the pole.
  • Distance along that path, and their sunrise/sunset in UTC
  • Set it by clicking the map, typing a grid or callsign + Enter, picking from DXCC, or clicking any spot. ★ = favorites, 🔒 = stop map clicks moving it.

5The map

  • Shaded side = night. Its edge is the gray line: low-band signals travel unusually far along it.
  • ☀ = where the sun is directly overhead; the moon icon = where the moon is.
  • Each line is a spot, from the station that heard to the station heard, colored by band (same colors everywhere in the app).
  • The band bar at the bottom filters the map to one band. The ticker is DX news.
  • Flat: everyday view; choose a basemap.
  • Azimuthal: centered on you; a straight line out from the center is your true beam heading.
  • 3D: a globe.
  • Left toolbar: zoom, lock, show/hide callsigns, night darkness.

3Solar pane: four views (button at top right; ▶ rotates them)

  • Image: live from NASA's Solar Dynamics Observatory. Dark patches are coronal holes, the source of fast solar wind that arrives 2–4 days later (watch K climb).
  • Indices: SFI, K, SSN, solar wind with a word rating, a trend, and a 24-hour K forecast.
  • Lunar: moon phase and position, for EME.
X-ray: flares. Classes B → C → M → X, each 10× the one before.
  • B, C: background, little effect.
  • M, X: HF radio blackout on the sunlit side of Earth, starting within minutes and lasting minutes to hours.

Bands die at midday? Check X-ray before you check the coax.

4Propagation pane: which bands, to there, and when

Chart view: a prediction for the path from your DE to your DX. Rows = bands, columns = 24 hours (UTC); the white box is now. Green = likely, yellow/orange = possible, red/dark = unlikely. It uses the ITU's HF model (ITU-R P.533, the physics behind VOACAP), calculated in your browser with your mode, power and antenna (default SSB, 100 W).

Example (FN20 → Tokyo, SSB, Oct 19): 40/30 m best ~08–14 UTC (our night); 17/15 m window ~21–00 UTC (5–8 PM EDT).

MUF and LUF

  • MUF, maximum usable frequency: the highest frequency the ionosphere typically bends back on this path now. Well above it, the signal goes into space.
  • LUF, lowest usable frequency: below it, daytime absorption eats the signal.
  • Bands between them can work; signal strength decides whether they do.
Chart view: FN20 → Tokyo, SSB, 100 W
Mode matters FT8 decodes far below the noise; SSB needs a signal well above it. Switch the mode to FT8 and "closed" bands often open.
General vs. your path "15 m GOOD" means open somewhere. The chart says whether your signal reaches that place. Use the first to decide whether to get on, the second to decide where to point.

Bars view: the same prediction for this hour, best band first.

Bands view: N0NBH's general GOOD / FAIR / POOR per band, day and night, plus VHF (aurora, sporadic-E) and noise.

6DX cluster

A spot is a report that a station was heard on a frequency, from other hams or from automatic receivers (the Reverse Beacon Network, RBN), shared over the worldwide DX cluster network.

Row: frequency (colored by band) · callsign · mode · de spotter · age.

  • Click a row: makes it your DX target and shows it on the map.
  • 🎧: listen on a nearby web receiver (KiwiSDR), already tuned.
  • 50/162: rows shown / spots received. Search and filters narrow it.
  • With your log imported: NEW new country · BAND new on this band · DUPE already worked · ✓ worked on another band/mode

7PSKReporter

Digital-mode software (WSJT-X, JTDX…) reports every station it decodes to PSKReporter.info.

  • ▲ Heard: stations that decoded you. ▼ Hearing: stations you decoded.
  • Numbers are SNR in dB. Gold ★ = you hear each other: a contact is likely.
  • "Waiting for spots… (TX to see reports)" = you haven't transmitted yet. Call CQ on FT8 for a few minutes and watch the map: a free antenna test.

8Programs and calendars

  • POTA Parks on the Air · SOTA Summits on the Air
  • WWFF World Wide Flora & Fauna · WWBOTA Bunkers on the Air
  • Row: activator · reference (e.g. US-11290 is a park; hover for its name) · frequency · UTC time · comment ("RBN" = heard by a skimmer)
  • DXpeditions (NG3K calendar): ● NOW on the air today; SOON coming up.
  • Contests (WA7BNM calendar): LIVE with time left; upcoming with start time.

Ten seconds before you turn the radio on

  1. Header: SFI up, K low?
  2. X-ray: any M or X flare? Day side may be blacked out.
  3. Propagation: set a DX target; read the chart for band and time.
  4. Map + cluster: is anyone actually getting through? Filter to that band.
  5. Listen: 🎧 on a spot, then tune the radio. POTA = easiest contacts.
